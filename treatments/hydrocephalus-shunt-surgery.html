What is Hydrocephalus?

Hydrocephalus is a condition in which cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) accumulates within the fluid-filled cavities (ventricles) of the brain. Under normal circumstances, CSF circulates continuously around the brain and spinal cord, providing cushioning, delivering nutrients, removing waste products, and helping maintain normal pressure inside the skull.

Hydrocephalus develops when this delicate balance is disrupted. The problem may occur because:

  • Too much cerebrospinal fluid is produced (rare)
  • The normal flow of CSF becomes blocked
  • The body cannot absorb CSF properly

As fluid continues to accumulate, the ventricles enlarge and pressure inside the skull increases. Without timely treatment, this increased intracranial pressure may damage delicate brain tissue and affect movement, memory, learning, vision, balance, and other neurological functions.

Modern Hydrocephalus Treatment in Bangalore focuses on restoring normal CSF circulation while protecting the brain from further injury.

Types of Hydrocephalus

Hydrocephalus is classified according to its underlying cause and the way cerebrospinal fluid circulation is affected.

01

Congenital Hydrocephalus

Congenital hydrocephalus is present at birth and develops because of abnormalities in the formation of the brain or cerebrospinal fluid pathways during pregnancy.

Common causes include:

  • Aqueductal stenosis
  • Spina bifida
  • Chiari malformation
  • Genetic abnormalities
  • Congenital brain malformations

Early diagnosis allows timely treatment and supports healthy neurological development.

02

Acquired Hydrocephalus

Acquired hydrocephalus develops after birth due to conditions that interfere with normal CSF circulation.

Common causes include:

  • Brain tumours
  • Head injuries
  • Brain haemorrhage
  • Meningitis
  • Stroke
  • Brain infections

Treatment depends on the underlying cause as well as the severity of the hydrocephalus.

03

Communicating Hydrocephalus

In communicating hydrocephalus, cerebrospinal fluid flows normally through the ventricles but is not absorbed efficiently into the bloodstream.

This type may occur after:

  • Brain infections
  • Subarachnoid haemorrhage
  • Brain surgery
  • Inflammatory neurological conditions
04

Obstructive (Non-Communicating) Hydrocephalus

Obstructive hydrocephalus develops when a blockage prevents CSF from flowing normally through the ventricular system.

Common causes include:

  • Brain tumours
  • Brain cysts
  • Congenital narrowing of the aqueduct (aqueductal stenosis)
  • Chiari malformation

Many patients with obstructive hydrocephalus may benefit from Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV), depending on the underlying anatomy.

05

Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)

Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus is a condition that primarily affects older adults. Although pressure measurements may appear relatively normal, excess CSF gradually enlarges the ventricles and affects brain function.

Patients often develop a characteristic combination of symptoms:

  • Difficulty walking
  • Memory problems
  • Urinary incontinence

Because these symptoms may resemble other neurological conditions, careful evaluation is essential. Early Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus Treatment in Bangalore can significantly improve quality of life in selected patients.

Causes and Risk Factors

Hydrocephalus may occur for several different reasons depending on the patient's age and underlying neurological condition.

Common causes include:

  • Congenital brain abnormalities
  • Brain tumours
  • Traumatic brain injury
  • Brain haemorrhage
  • Stroke
  • Meningitis
  • Brain cysts
  • Aqueductal stenosis
  • Chiari malformation
  • Previous brain surgery
  • Age-related neurological disorders

Some patients develop hydrocephalus gradually, while others experience sudden symptoms requiring urgent neurosurgical evaluation.

Symptoms of Hydrocephalus

The symptoms of hydrocephalus vary depending on the patient's age, the severity of the condition, and how rapidly pressure builds within the brain.

Symptoms in Infants

Infants may develop:

  • Rapid increase in head size
  • Bulging soft spot (fontanelle)
  • Poor feeding
  • Irritability
  • Vomiting
  • Excessive sleepiness
  • Downward deviation of the eyes ("sun-setting" sign)
  • Seizures

Symptoms in Children

Children may experience:

  • Persistent headaches
  • Morning vomiting
  • Difficulty walking
  • Poor balance
  • Vision problems
  • Learning difficulties
  • Developmental delay
  • Behavioural changes
  • Seizures

Symptoms in Adults

Adults commonly experience:

  • Persistent headaches
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Blurred vision
  • Balance problems
  • Memory difficulties
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • Drowsiness
  • Weakness
  • Changes in personality

Symptoms of Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus

Patients with NPH often develop:

  • Slow, shuffling gait
  • Difficulty maintaining balance
  • Memory impairment
  • Urinary urgency or incontinence

These symptoms can overlap with other neurological conditions, making specialist evaluation particularly important.

Observation and Conservative Management

Not all patients diagnosed with hydrocephalus require immediate surgery.

Observation may be appropriate for:

  • Mild ventricular enlargement without symptoms
  • Stable hydrocephalus identified incidentally
  • Patients with minimal neurological deficits
  • Selected cases under regular neurological monitoring

During observation, patients undergo periodic neurological examinations and repeat MRI or CT scans to monitor ventricular size and detect any progression of the condition.

Medical Management

Although medications cannot cure hydrocephalus, they may help manage associated symptoms or treat underlying conditions while surgical planning is underway.

Medical treatment may include:

  • Management of infections
  • Control of seizures when present
  • Pain management
  • Treatment of associated neurological disorders
  • Supportive care before surgery

However, when cerebrospinal fluid continues to accumulate and pressure inside the brain increases, surgery is usually required to restore normal CSF circulation.

VP Shunt Surgery

Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) Shunt Surgery is the most commonly performed procedure for treating hydrocephalus.

A VP shunt is a specialized drainage system that diverts excess cerebrospinal fluid from the brain's ventricles to the abdominal cavity, where it is safely absorbed by the body.

The system consists of:

  • A ventricular catheter placed inside the brain
  • A pressure-regulating valve
  • A flexible catheter that carries CSF to the abdomen

This controlled drainage relieves pressure on the brain and helps prevent further neurological damage.

Patients looking for VP Shunt Surgery Bangalore should undergo a detailed evaluation to determine the most appropriate shunt type and valve setting based on their individual condition.

CSF Shunt Surgery

CSF Shunt Surgery refers to procedures that redirect excess cerebrospinal fluid away from the brain to another part of the body where it can be absorbed naturally.

Several types of shunt systems are available depending on the patient's condition.

These include:

  • Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunt
  • Ventriculoatrial (VA) shunt
  • Ventriculopleural shunt

Among these, the VP shunt remains the most widely used because of its excellent long-term safety and effectiveness.

Programmable Shunt Surgery

Modern programmable shunts allow neurosurgeons to adjust the drainage pressure externally without requiring another operation.

Benefits of Programmable Shunt Surgery include:

  • Adjustable pressure settings
  • Personalized CSF drainage
  • Reduced need for repeat surgery
  • Improved symptom control
  • Greater flexibility during long-term follow-up

Not every patient requires a programmable valve. Dr. Sachin recommends the most appropriate shunt system based on the patient's age, diagnosis, and clinical condition.

Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV)

Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV) is an advanced minimally invasive procedure used to treat selected patients with obstructive hydrocephalus.

Instead of implanting a shunt, a neuroendoscope is used to create a small opening in the floor of the third ventricle, allowing cerebrospinal fluid to bypass the blockage and flow through its natural pathways.

Patients searching for Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV) Bangalore may be suitable candidates if imaging demonstrates obstructive hydrocephalus and favourable ventricular anatomy.

Potential advantages of ETV include:

  • No permanent shunt implantation
  • Reduced risk of shunt-related complications
  • More physiological CSF circulation
  • Faster recovery in selected patients

However, ETV is not suitable for every patient. Careful clinical and radiological evaluation is essential before recommending this procedure.

Hydrocephalus Treatment in Children and Infants

Hydrocephalus can develop during infancy or childhood because of congenital abnormalities, infections, bleeding, or brain tumours.

Early diagnosis is particularly important because prolonged pressure on the developing brain may affect neurological development, learning, vision, and motor skills.

Treatment depends on the underlying cause and may include:

Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH) Treatment

Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus Treatment in Bangalore requires careful assessment because its symptoms often resemble Parkinson's disease, Alzheimer's disease, or other age-related neurological disorders.

After confirming the diagnosis through clinical evaluation and imaging, treatment may include:

Appropriately selected patients often experience meaningful improvement in walking ability, balance, and bladder control after treatment.

When is Surgery Recommended?

Hydrocephalus surgery is recommended when excess cerebrospinal fluid causes neurological symptoms or progressive enlargement of the ventricles.

Surgery may be advised if the patient develops:

  • Persistent headaches
  • Progressive vomiting
  • Enlarging ventricles on MRI or CT
  • Vision problems
  • Developmental delay
  • Memory impairment
  • Difficulty walking
  • Seizures
  • Raised intracranial pressure
  • Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus with functional impairment

The choice between VP Shunt Surgery and Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV) depends on the underlying cause of hydrocephalus, ventricular anatomy, patient age, and overall neurological condition.

What is hydrocephalus?

Hydrocephalus is a condition in which excess cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) accumulates inside the brain's ventricles, increasing pressure within the skull and affecting normal brain function.

What causes hydrocephalus?

Hydrocephalus may develop due to congenital abnormalities, brain tumours, traumatic brain injury, meningitis, stroke, brain haemorrhage, cysts, or age-related disorders such as Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus.

What are the symptoms of hydrocephalus?

Symptoms vary with age but may include persistent headaches, vomiting, vision changes, balance problems, developmental delay in children, enlarged head size in infants, memory problems, urinary incontinence, and difficulty walking.

What is VP Shunt Surgery?

VP Shunt Surgery involves placing a ventriculoperitoneal shunt that diverts excess cerebrospinal fluid from the brain to the abdominal cavity, where it is naturally absorbed, relieving pressure inside the brain.

What is Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV)?

Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV) is a minimally invasive procedure in which a small opening is created in the floor of the third ventricle to restore normal CSF flow without implanting a permanent shunt. It is suitable for selected patients with obstructive hydrocephalus.

What is a programmable shunt?

A programmable shunt contains an adjustable valve that allows the neurosurgeon to modify the drainage pressure externally without additional surgery, providing greater flexibility in long-term hydrocephalus management.

Can hydrocephalus occur in adults?

Yes. Hydrocephalus can affect adults due to brain tumours, head injuries, infections, haemorrhage, or conditions such as Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus. Adults may experience headaches, balance problems, memory impairment, or walking difficulties.

Is hydrocephalus treatment permanent?

Many patients experience long-term improvement after treatment. However, patients with shunts require regular follow-up because shunt systems may occasionally require adjustment or revision over time.

How long is recovery after hydrocephalus surgery?

Recovery varies depending on the patient's age, overall health, and the procedure performed. Many patients gradually resume normal daily activities within a few weeks, while neurological improvement may continue over several months.

What are the signs of a shunt malfunction?

Possible signs include severe headache, nausea, vomiting, excessive sleepiness, vision changes, balance problems, seizures, swelling along the shunt pathway, or worsening neurological symptoms. Immediate medical evaluation is recommended if these occur.

When should I seek a second opinion?

A second opinion is valuable if surgery has been recommended, symptoms persist despite treatment, or you wish to better understand your diagnosis and available treatment options before proceeding.
