What is a Glioma?

A glioma is a type of brain tumour that develops from glial cells, which are the supportive cells of the brain and spinal cord. These cells help protect, nourish, and maintain the function of nerve cells. When glial cells begin to grow abnormally, they form a tumour known as a glioma.

Gliomas are among the most common primary brain tumours seen in adults, although they can also occur in children. Some grow slowly over several years, while others are more aggressive and require immediate treatment. Because they arise within the brain tissue, they often require specialized care from an experienced glioma specialist and a multidisciplinary neuro-oncology team.

Early diagnosis plays a crucial role in planning treatment, reducing symptoms, and improving long-term outcomes.

Types of Glioma

Gliomas are classified according to the type of glial cell involved and their biological behaviour.

01

Astrocytoma

Astrocytomas arise from astrocytes and may range from slow-growing tumours to aggressive forms such as glioblastoma.

02

Oligodendroglioma

These tumours develop from oligodendrocytes, the cells responsible for producing myelin, the protective covering around nerve fibres. They generally grow more slowly but still require expert evaluation.

03

Ependymoma

Ependymomas originate from the cells lining the ventricles of the brain and the spinal canal. They are less common but can occur in both adults and children.

04

Glioblastoma

Glioblastoma is the most aggressive type of glioma. It usually requires a combination of glioma brain surgery, radiation therapy, and chemotherapy for comprehensive treatment.

What Causes Glioma?

The exact cause of most gliomas is still unknown. In many patients, no single cause can be identified. However, research has shown that certain factors may increase the risk of developing a glioma.

These include:

  • Certain inherited genetic syndromes
  • Previous exposure to radiation therapy involving the head
  • Increasing age
  • Rare family history of brain tumours
  • Specific genetic and molecular alterations within brain cells

Current evidence has not confirmed that routine mobile phone use directly causes glioma. Most patients diagnosed with glioma have no identifiable risk factors.

Signs and Symptoms of Glioma

The symptoms of a glioma depend on its size, location, and rate of growth. Some patients experience gradual neurological changes, while others may develop symptoms suddenly.

Common symptoms include:

  • Persistent or worsening headaches
  • Seizures
  • Weakness or numbness in the arms or legs
  • Difficulty speaking or understanding speech
  • Memory loss or confusion
  • Vision problems
  • Balance and coordination difficulties
  • Personality or behavioural changes
  • Nausea and vomiting

The location of the tumour also influences the symptoms. For example, tumours in the frontal lobe may affect behaviour and movement, while tumours in the temporal lobe can interfere with memory and speech. Gliomas involving the cerebellum often cause balance problems, whereas brainstem tumours may affect swallowing, facial movements, or coordination.

Persistent neurological symptoms should never be ignored, especially if they continue to worsen over time.

When Should You Consult a Neurosurgeon?

Although headaches and dizziness are common complaints, persistent neurological symptoms require prompt medical evaluation. Consulting a neurosurgeon early allows timely diagnosis and helps determine whether further investigations or treatment are required.

You should seek specialist consultation if you experience:

  • New-onset seizures
  • Persistent headaches that continue to worsen
  • Sudden weakness in an arm or leg
  • Difficulty speaking or understanding language
  • Changes in vision
  • Problems with balance or walking
  • Memory loss or personality changes
  • Recurrent nausea and vomiting associated with neurological symptoms

Early diagnosis often allows more treatment options and may improve both neurological recovery and long-term outcomes.

Diagnosis of Glioma

An accurate diagnosis is the foundation of successful glioma treatment. Every patient undergoes a detailed evaluation before any treatment decision is made.

The diagnostic process usually includes a neurological examination to assess muscle strength, sensation, reflexes, coordination, vision, speech, and memory. This helps determine which part of the brain may be affected.

MRI of the brain is the most important investigation for diagnosing glioma. It provides detailed information about the tumour's size, location, relationship to surrounding brain tissue, and possible involvement of nearby structures. In certain situations, CT scans, Functional MRI, MR Angiography, or PET scans may also be recommended to obtain additional information.

When required, a brain biopsy is performed to confirm the diagnosis. The tissue sample is analysed to determine the tumour type, WHO grade, and important molecular markers such as IDH mutation and MGMT status. These findings help guide treatment decisions and predict how the tumour may respond to therapy.

After reviewing the clinical findings, imaging studies, and pathology results, Dr. Sachin develops a personalized treatment plan tailored to the patient's specific condition.

Treatment Options for Glioma

There is no single treatment suitable for every patient with a glioma. The treatment approach depends on several factors, including the tumour's grade, size, location, molecular characteristics, symptoms, and overall health of the patient.

Management may include:

  • Careful observation for selected low-grade tumours
  • Glioma surgery to remove as much of the tumour as safely possible
  • Radiation therapy
  • Chemotherapy
  • Targeted therapies for selected patients
  • Rehabilitation and supportive care

In most patients, glioma surgical treatment plays a central role because it helps relieve pressure within the brain, improves symptoms, provides tissue for diagnosis, and creates the best possible foundation for additional treatments when required.

Dr. Sachin works closely with neuro-oncologists, radiation oncologists, neuroradiologists, neuropathologists, and rehabilitation specialists to ensure every patient receives comprehensive multidisciplinary care.

When is Glioma Surgery Recommended?

Not every glioma requires immediate surgery. The decision depends on the tumour's type, grade, location, size, symptoms, and its effect on surrounding brain tissue. The primary goal is to choose the treatment that offers the best balance between tumour control and preservation of neurological function.

Glioma surgery is usually recommended when:

  • The tumour is causing seizures or persistent headaches.
  • There is progressive weakness, speech difficulty, or vision changes.
  • MRI shows tumour growth.
  • A tissue diagnosis is needed to determine the tumour type and grade.
  • The tumour is causing increased pressure within the brain.
  • Maximum safe tumour removal is expected to improve treatment outcomes.

In selected patients with very small, slow-growing tumours that are not causing symptoms, careful observation with regular MRI scans may be appropriate. Dr. Sachin evaluates every patient individually before recommending surgery or any other form of glioma treatment in Bangalore.

Glioma Surgery

Surgery remains one of the most effective treatments for many gliomas and is often the first step in managing the disease. The objective is to remove as much of the tumour as safely possible while protecting the healthy brain tissue responsible for movement, speech, memory, and other essential functions.

The benefits of glioma brain tumour surgery include:

  • Reducing pressure within the brain
  • Relieving neurological symptoms
  • Improving seizure control
  • Obtaining tissue for accurate diagnosis
  • Supporting further treatment such as radiation therapy or chemotherapy
  • Improving long-term disease control in suitable patients

The extent of tumour removal depends on the tumour's location and its relationship to critical brain structures. Dr. Sachin focuses on maximum safe resection, ensuring that tumour removal is balanced with the preservation of neurological function.

Surgical Techniques Used for Glioma Treatment

01

Craniotomy

Craniotomy is the most commonly performed procedure for glioma removal surgery. During the operation, a small section of the skull is temporarily removed to allow access to the tumour. Using high-powered operating microscopes and microsurgical instruments, the tumour is removed with precision before the bone flap is replaced.

02

Awake Brain Surgery

When a glioma is located close to areas responsible for speech, language, or movement, Awake Brain Surgery may be recommended.

During specific stages of the procedure, the patient remains awake so that important brain functions can be monitored in real time. This allows the surgeon to remove the tumour more safely while reducing the risk of permanent neurological deficits.

03

Image-Guided Microsurgery

Modern glioma surgical treatment combines microsurgery with neuronavigation technology. MRI and CT images act as a navigation system, helping the surgeon identify the tumour accurately and avoid critical brain structures during surgery.

This approach improves surgical precision while minimizing damage to healthy tissue.

Advanced Technology for Safer Brain Surgery

Successful glioma treatment depends not only on surgical expertise but also on the use of modern technology that improves accuracy and patient safety.

Neuronavigation

Neuronavigation provides a three-dimensional map of the brain during surgery, allowing accurate localization of the tumour and helping the surgeon navigate safely around important neurological structures.

Brain Mapping

When tumours are located near areas controlling speech or movement, brain mapping helps identify functional brain regions so that these areas can be preserved during tumour removal.

Intraoperative Neuromonitoring

Continuous monitoring of nerve pathways during surgery helps detect changes in neurological function immediately, improving patient safety during complex procedures.

Operating Microscope

High-definition operating microscopes provide excellent magnification, allowing delicate tumour removal while protecting healthy blood vessels, nerves, and surrounding brain tissue.

These technologies work together to improve the safety and effectiveness of glioma brain surgery, particularly for tumours located in complex areas of the brain.

Risks and Possible Complications

Like all major brain surgeries, glioma brain surgery carries certain risks. These vary depending on the tumour's location, size, and complexity.

Potential complications may include:

  • Bleeding
  • Infection
  • Brain swelling
  • Seizures
  • Cerebrospinal fluid leak
  • Temporary or permanent neurological deficits
  • Stroke
  • Anaesthesia-related complications

Before recommending surgery, Dr. Sachin discusses the expected benefits and potential risks in detail. Careful surgical planning, advanced imaging, neuronavigation, and intraoperative monitoring are used to reduce complications wherever possible.

Recovery After Glioma Surgery

Recovery varies from patient to patient and depends on the complexity of the procedure and the individual's neurological condition.

Immediately after surgery, patients are monitored in the intensive care unit before being shifted to a regular ward. Recovery usually includes pain management, neurological assessment, wound care, early mobilization, and repeat imaging when required.

Depending on the pathology results, additional treatments such as radiation therapy or chemotherapy may be recommended.

Most patients gradually return to daily activities under medical supervision while continuing regular follow-up and MRI surveillance.

Rehabilitation After Glioma Treatment

Recovery does not end after surgery. Rehabilitation is an important part of glioma cancer treatment, helping patients regain independence and improve quality of life.

Depending on individual needs, rehabilitation may include:

  • Physiotherapy
  • Occupational therapy
  • Speech and language therapy
  • Cognitive rehabilitation
  • Balance and coordination training
  • Psychological support

A structured rehabilitation programme helps patients recover physically, emotionally, and cognitively while adapting to life after treatment.

Why Early Diagnosis Matters

Gliomas often begin with symptoms that may appear mild or be mistaken for common neurological conditions. Delaying medical evaluation may allow the tumour to grow, making treatment more complex and increasing the risk of neurological impairment.

Early diagnosis offers several advantages:

  • More treatment options
  • Better surgical planning
  • Greater likelihood of maximum safe tumour removal
  • Improved preservation of brain function
  • Earlier symptom relief
  • Better long-term outcomes

If you experience persistent headaches, seizures, weakness, changes in speech, vision, balance, or memory, seeking an early consultation with a neurosurgeon can make a significant difference.

What is a glioma?

A glioma is a brain tumour that develops from glial cells, which support and protect nerve cells in the brain and spinal cord. Gliomas may be slow-growing or aggressive, depending on their type and grade.

Is every glioma cancerous?

No. Some gliomas are low-grade and grow slowly, while others are high-grade and behave more aggressively. The tumour grade helps determine the most appropriate treatment plan.

What is the best treatment for glioma?

Treatment depends on the tumour's type, grade, size, and location. It may include surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or a combination of these approaches.

When is glioma surgery recommended?

Surgery is recommended when the tumour is causing symptoms, growing, increasing pressure within the brain, or when a tissue diagnosis is required. The goal is to remove as much of the tumour as safely possible while preserving neurological function.

Is glioma surgery safe?

Modern neurosurgical techniques and technologies such as neuronavigation, brain mapping, and intraoperative neuromonitoring have significantly improved the safety and precision of glioma surgery. The risks vary depending on the tumour's location and complexity.

Can all gliomas be completely removed?

Not always. Some tumours are located close to critical brain structures or infiltrate healthy brain tissue. In such cases, the aim is to achieve maximum safe tumour removal while protecting essential neurological functions.

What is Awake Brain Surgery?

Awake Brain Surgery is performed for selected tumours located near areas controlling speech or movement. Patients are awake during specific stages of the procedure so that these functions can be monitored while the tumour is removed.

Will I need radiation therapy or chemotherapy after surgery?

Depending on the tumour's pathology, WHO grade, and molecular characteristics, additional treatment such as radiation therapy, chemotherapy, or targeted therapy may be recommended after surgery.

How long does recovery take after glioma surgery?

Recovery varies depending on the complexity of the surgery and the patient's neurological condition. Many patients gradually resume normal activities over several weeks, while others may require additional rehabilitation.

Can gliomas return after treatment?

Some gliomas have a risk of recurrence. Regular follow-up with MRI scans and neurological evaluations is important for monitoring recovery and detecting any changes early.

When should I seek a second opinion?

A second opinion is recommended if you have been diagnosed with a glioma, advised to undergo surgery, or would like to better understand your treatment options before making a decision.
