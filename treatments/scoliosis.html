What is Scoliosis?

Scoliosis is a three-dimensional deformity of the spine in which the vertebral column curves sideways and rotates simultaneously. Instead of appearing straight when viewed from behind, the spine develops an "S-shaped" or "C-shaped" curve.

A healthy spine naturally has gentle curves when viewed from the side. In scoliosis, however, the abnormal sideways curvature changes the alignment of the spine and may affect the shoulders, rib cage, waist, and pelvis. As the condition progresses, it can alter posture, reduce spinal flexibility, and place uneven stress on the muscles and joints.

Although scoliosis commonly develops during childhood or adolescence, it can also occur in adults due to age-related degeneration, previous spinal conditions, or neuromuscular disorders.

The primary goal of scoliosis treatment is to prevent progression of the curve, reduce symptoms, maintain spinal balance, and preserve normal function.

Types of Scoliosis

Understanding the type of scoliosis is essential because treatment varies depending on the underlying cause and the patient's age.

01

Idiopathic Scoliosis

Idiopathic scoliosis is the most common type, accounting for the majority of cases. The exact cause is unknown, although genetic factors are believed to play a role. It most commonly develops during adolescence while the spine is still growing.

02

Congenital Scoliosis

Congenital scoliosis develops before birth due to abnormal formation of the vertebrae. The severity varies depending on the type of vertebral abnormality and may require early monitoring or surgical correction.

03

Neuromuscular Scoliosis

This form of scoliosis develops secondary to neurological or muscular disorders such as cerebral palsy, muscular dystrophy, or spinal cord abnormalities. These patients often require multidisciplinary management.

04

Degenerative Adult Scoliosis

Adult scoliosis may develop due to age-related degeneration of the spinal discs and joints. Patients commonly experience back pain, nerve compression, leg pain, and difficulty walking.

05

Syndromic Scoliosis

Some genetic syndromes are associated with scoliosis as part of a broader pattern of musculoskeletal abnormalities. Treatment depends on the underlying condition and the severity of the spinal deformity.

Causes and Risk Factors

The exact cause of scoliosis depends on its type. While many cases have no clearly identifiable cause, several factors increase the likelihood of developing spinal curvature.

Common causes and risk factors include:

  • Genetic predisposition
  • Congenital vertebral abnormalities
  • Neuromuscular disorders
  • Degenerative changes of the spine
  • Previous spinal injuries
  • Connective tissue disorders
  • Increasing age (degenerative scoliosis)

Most cases of adolescent idiopathic scoliosis develop without any identifiable injury or lifestyle-related cause.

Symptoms of Scoliosis

The symptoms of scoliosis vary depending on the severity of the spinal curve and the patient's age.

Common symptoms include:

  • Uneven shoulders
  • One shoulder blade appearing more prominent
  • Uneven waistline
  • One hip positioned higher than the other
  • Visible spinal curvature
  • Back pain
  • Neck pain
  • Muscle fatigue
  • Difficulty standing for long periods
  • Reduced spinal flexibility
  • Numbness or weakness in the legs (in selected adult cases)
  • Difficulty walking in severe degenerative scoliosis

Large spinal curves may also affect chest expansion and breathing by reducing the space available for the lungs.

Diagnosis of Scoliosis

Accurate diagnosis is the first step in planning successful scoliosis treatment in Bangalore.

01

Clinical Evaluation

The consultation begins with a detailed medical history and physical examination. Dr. Sachin evaluates posture, shoulder balance, spinal alignment, pelvic position, neurological function, walking pattern, and flexibility.

02

Adam's Forward Bend Test

This simple clinical examination helps identify spinal rotation and rib prominence, which are characteristic features of scoliosis.

03

Standing Spine X-rays

Standing full-length spine X-rays remain the most important investigation for diagnosing scoliosis. These images help measure the spinal curve and evaluate overall spinal balance.

04

Cobb Angle Measurement

The Cobb angle is the internationally accepted method for measuring scoliosis severity.

Generally:

  • Mild scoliosis: Less than 20 degrees
  • Moderate scoliosis: 20-40 degrees
  • Severe scoliosis: Greater than 40-50 degrees

Treatment recommendations are based on the Cobb angle, symptoms, skeletal maturity, and risk of progression rather than the X-ray alone.

05

MRI Spine

MRI may be recommended when neurological symptoms are present or when congenital abnormalities, spinal cord disorders, or other underlying conditions are suspected.

06

CT Scan

CT imaging may be required in selected patients to evaluate complex spinal anatomy before surgery.

Observation and Conservative Treatment

Not every patient diagnosed with scoliosis requires surgery. Small spinal curves that are stable and do not cause symptoms can often be managed conservatively.

Observation may be recommended when:

  • The spinal curve is mild.
  • There is minimal risk of progression.
  • The patient has completed skeletal growth.
  • There are no neurological symptoms.
  • Daily activities are not significantly affected.

Regular follow-up with clinical examinations and standing X-rays helps monitor the curvature and detect any progression early.

01

Physiotherapy and Rehabilitation

Physiotherapy plays an important role in improving posture, muscle strength, flexibility, and overall spinal function.

Although physiotherapy cannot permanently straighten a structural scoliosis curve, it helps:

  • Improve posture
  • Strengthen core muscles
  • Reduce muscle imbalance
  • Improve flexibility
  • Manage pain
  • Enhance overall spinal stability

Scoliosis-specific exercise programmes may be recommended as part of comprehensive conservative treatment.

02

Bracing

For children and adolescents whose spines are still growing, spinal bracing may help reduce the risk of curve progression.

Bracing is usually considered when:

  • The Cobb angle falls within the appropriate treatment range.
  • Significant spinal growth remains.
  • There is a high likelihood of curve progression.

Bracing aims to prevent further worsening of the spinal curve and may reduce the need for surgery in selected patients.

When is Scoliosis Surgery Recommended?

Scoliosis surgery is considered when the spinal curve progresses despite conservative treatment or when the deformity begins to affect daily life, neurological function, or lung capacity.

Scoliosis surgery in Bangalore may be recommended when:

  • The Cobb angle continues to increase.
  • The curve becomes severe.
  • Significant spinal imbalance develops.
  • Persistent back pain affects quality of life.
  • Neurological symptoms occur due to nerve compression.
  • Breathing is affected in severe deformities.
  • Conservative treatment has failed to control progression.
  • Progressive deformity causes cosmetic or functional concerns.

The decision to proceed with surgery is based on a comprehensive evaluation that includes clinical findings, imaging studies, skeletal maturity, and the patient's individual goals.

Modern Surgical Techniques for Scoliosis Correction

Advances in spinal surgery have significantly improved the safety and effectiveness of scoliosis correction.

01

Posterior Spinal Fusion

Posterior spinal fusion is the most commonly performed procedure for scoliosis correction. Through an incision over the back, the spine is carefully realigned and stabilized using specialized implants while the affected vertebrae fuse into a stable position over time.

02

Pedicle Screw Instrumentation

Modern pedicle screw systems provide strong fixation, allowing controlled correction of spinal deformity while maintaining spinal stability.

These implants help:

  • Correct spinal alignment
  • Improve balance
  • Maintain correction during healing
  • Support spinal fusion
03

Spinal Deformity Correction

Every scoliosis curve is unique. Surgical correction is planned carefully to improve alignment while preserving neurological safety.

Rather than aiming for complete straightening, the focus is on achieving a balanced spine that functions well and provides long-term stability.

Advanced Technologies Used During Surgery

Successful scoliosis surgery in Bangalore depends on advanced planning, precision, and continuous neurological protection.

Computer-Assisted Surgical Planning

Detailed imaging studies help determine the optimal surgical strategy, implant placement, and correction required for each patient.

Intraoperative Neuromonitoring

Continuous monitoring of the spinal cord and nerves throughout surgery helps detect neurological changes immediately, improving patient safety during deformity correction.

Image-Guided Navigation

Navigation technology assists with accurate implant placement and improves surgical precision, particularly in complex spinal deformities.

High-Speed Precision Instruments

Modern surgical instruments allow accurate bone preparation and implant placement while minimizing trauma to surrounding tissues.

Risks and Possible Complications

Like all major spinal procedures, scoliosis surgery carries certain risks. The likelihood of complications depends on the severity of the deformity, overall health, and complexity of the procedure.

Potential risks include:

  • Bleeding
  • Infection
  • Nerve injury
  • Implant-related complications
  • Failure of spinal fusion
  • Curve progression
  • Adjacent segment degeneration
  • Anaesthesia-related complications

Comprehensive preoperative planning, advanced surgical techniques, and intraoperative neuromonitoring help reduce these risks.

Recovery After Scoliosis Surgery

Recovery is gradual and varies depending on the patient's age, the extent of correction, and overall health.

During Hospital Stay

Patients are closely monitored for neurological function, pain control, wound healing, and early mobilization. Physiotherapy begins under medical supervision to encourage safe movement.

First Two Weeks

Walking is encouraged, while heavy lifting, bending, and twisting should be avoided. Pain gradually improves, and patients become more independent with daily activities.

Six to Twelve Weeks

Most patients return to school, office work, or light activities after medical clearance. Gentle physiotherapy continues to improve strength and mobility.

Three to Twelve Months

Spinal fusion continues to mature over several months. During this period, activity levels are gradually increased under medical guidance until normal function is restored.

Regular follow-up appointments and imaging help monitor healing and ensure that spinal correction is maintained.

What is scoliosis?

Scoliosis is a condition in which the spine develops an abnormal sideways curvature accompanied by rotation of the vertebrae. The severity of the curve varies from mild to severe and may affect posture, balance, and overall spinal function.

What causes scoliosis?

The cause depends on the type of scoliosis. It may be idiopathic (unknown cause), congenital, neuromuscular, degenerative, or associated with certain genetic conditions. Idiopathic scoliosis is the most common type.

Does every patient with scoliosis require surgery?

No. Many patients with mild scoliosis can be managed with observation, physiotherapy, or bracing. Surgery is recommended only when the curve progresses significantly, causes symptoms, or affects spinal balance and function.

What is the Cobb angle?

The Cobb angle is the standard method used to measure the degree of spinal curvature on X-rays. It helps determine the severity of scoliosis and guides treatment decisions.

When is scoliosis surgery recommended?

Surgery is usually considered when the spinal curve continues to progress despite conservative treatment, becomes severe, causes significant pain, neurological symptoms, breathing difficulties, or affects daily activities.

Is scoliosis surgery safe?

Modern scoliosis surgery is performed using advanced techniques, image-guided planning, and intraoperative neuromonitoring to improve safety. As with any major surgery, the risks depend on the patient's condition and the complexity of the deformity.

Can adults undergo scoliosis surgery?

Yes. Adults with degenerative scoliosis or progressive spinal deformity may benefit from surgery when conservative treatment fails and symptoms significantly affect quality of life.

How long does recovery take after scoliosis surgery?

Recovery varies depending on the extent of the surgery and the patient's overall health. Most patients gradually return to routine daily activities over several weeks, while complete spinal fusion and recovery continue over several months.

Can scoliosis come back after surgery?

Modern scoliosis correction aims to provide long-term spinal stability. While the corrected curve usually remains stable, regular follow-up is important to monitor spinal health and identify any rare complications.

Can I return to sports after scoliosis treatment?

Many patients are able to return to sports and physical activities after completing rehabilitation and receiving clearance from their surgeon. The timing depends on the type of treatment and individual recovery.

When should I seek a second opinion?

A second opinion is recommended if you have been diagnosed with scoliosis, advised to undergo surgery, or wish to understand all available treatment options before making a decision.
