What is a Brain Aneurysm?

A brain aneurysm, also known as a cerebral aneurysm, is an abnormal bulging or ballooning of a weakened area in the wall of a brain artery. As blood flows through the artery, pressure on the weakened vessel wall may cause the aneurysm to enlarge over time.

Many aneurysms remain stable for years without causing symptoms. However, larger aneurysms or those with a higher risk of rupture require careful monitoring or treatment. If an aneurysm ruptures, blood leaks into the space surrounding the brain, resulting in a subarachnoid haemorrhage, a life-threatening neurological emergency that requires immediate treatment.

Modern Brain Aneurysm Treatment in Bangalore aims to prevent rupture in high-risk aneurysms or stop further bleeding if rupture has already occurred.

Types of Brain Aneurysms

Brain aneurysms are classified based on their shape and underlying cause. Understanding the type of aneurysm helps determine the most appropriate treatment approach.

01

Saccular (Berry) Aneurysm

The saccular aneurysm is the most common type of brain aneurysm. It appears as a small, rounded pouch arising from one side of a blood vessel and is frequently found near the base of the brain.

Most aneurysm clipping and coiling procedures are performed for this type of aneurysm.

02

Fusiform Aneurysm

A fusiform aneurysm involves widening of the entire circumference of a blood vessel rather than forming a small pouch.

Treatment depends on:

  • Size
  • Location
  • Symptoms
  • Blood flow characteristics
03

Dissecting Aneurysm

A dissecting aneurysm develops when a tear forms within the wall of an artery, allowing blood to enter between its layers.

Although less common, these aneurysms may require urgent treatment because of their potential to reduce blood flow to the brain or rupture.

04

Mycotic Aneurysm

Mycotic aneurysms occur as a result of infection affecting the arterial wall.

Treatment usually combines antibiotics with neurosurgical or endovascular intervention when necessary.

Causes and Risk Factors

The exact cause of a brain aneurysm is not always known, but several factors increase the likelihood of developing one.

Common risk factors include:

  • High blood pressure (hypertension)
  • Smoking
  • Family history of brain aneurysms
  • Increasing age
  • Female gender
  • Connective tissue disorders
  • Polycystic kidney disease
  • Previous brain aneurysm
  • Head injury (less common)
  • Certain congenital blood vessel abnormalities

Managing modifiable risk factors such as hypertension and smoking can help reduce the likelihood of aneurysm growth or rupture.

Symptoms of a Brain Aneurysm

Symptoms depend on whether the aneurysm is unruptured or has ruptured.

Symptoms of an Unruptured Brain Aneurysm

Many unruptured aneurysms produce no symptoms and are discovered incidentally during MRI or CT scans.

Larger aneurysms may cause:

  • Persistent headaches
  • Eye pain
  • Double vision
  • Blurred vision
  • Drooping eyelid
  • Facial numbness
  • Difficulty with eye movements

Symptoms of a Ruptured Brain Aneurysm

A ruptured aneurysm is a medical emergency.

Symptoms often develop suddenly and may include:

  • Sudden, severe headache ("worst headache of life")
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Neck stiffness
  • Vomiting
  • Seizures
  • Sensitivity to light
  • Confusion
  • Difficulty speaking
  • Weakness or paralysis
  • Blurred or double vision

Anyone experiencing these symptoms requires immediate emergency medical attention.

Diagnosis of Brain Aneurysm

Accurate diagnosis is essential for successful Brain Aneurysm Treatment in Bangalore. The evaluation focuses on confirming the presence of an aneurysm, assessing its size and location, determining whether it has ruptured, and identifying the most appropriate treatment approach.

01

Clinical Evaluation

Dr. Sachin begins with a detailed medical history and neurological examination to assess symptoms, identify risk factors, and evaluate brain function.

The assessment includes:

  • Headache history
  • Vision assessment
  • Cranial nerve examination
  • Muscle strength
  • Sensation
  • Balance and coordination
  • Speech evaluation
  • Family history of aneurysms or stroke

This evaluation helps determine the urgency of treatment and guides further investigations.

02

CT Brain

A CT scan is often the first imaging test performed in emergency situations, particularly when a ruptured aneurysm is suspected.

It helps detect:

  • Bleeding around the brain (subarachnoid haemorrhage)
  • Brain swelling
  • Hydrocephalus
  • Acute neurological complications

CT imaging allows rapid diagnosis and supports immediate treatment planning.

03

MRI Brain

MRI provides highly detailed images of the brain and surrounding structures. It is particularly useful for evaluating unruptured aneurysms, identifying associated brain abnormalities, and planning treatment.

MRI may also be combined with Magnetic Resonance Angiography (MRA) to visualize blood vessels without invasive procedures.

04

CT Angiography (CTA)

CT Angiography uses contrast dye to produce detailed images of the brain's arteries.

It helps determine:

  • Size of the aneurysm
  • Shape of the aneurysm
  • Exact location
  • Relationship to nearby blood vessels

CTA plays an important role in deciding whether clipping or coiling is the most appropriate treatment.

05

Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA)

Digital Subtraction Angiography is considered the gold standard for evaluating brain aneurysms.

It provides highly detailed images of the cerebral circulation and is often performed before surgical or endovascular treatment to precisely map the aneurysm and surrounding blood vessels.

When is Brain Aneurysm Treatment Recommended?

Not every brain aneurysm requires immediate surgery. Some small, stable aneurysms with a low risk of rupture can be monitored with regular imaging and neurological follow-up. However, treatment is recommended when the benefits of intervention outweigh the risks of observation.

Brain Aneurysm Treatment in Bangalore may be advised if:

  • The aneurysm has ruptured.
  • The aneurysm is increasing in size.
  • The aneurysm is large or has an irregular shape.
  • Symptoms such as headaches, vision changes, or cranial nerve compression are present.
  • There is a strong family history of aneurysm rupture.
  • The aneurysm is located in a high-risk blood vessel.
  • The patient is young with a long life expectancy.
  • Imaging suggests an increased risk of rupture.

Every treatment decision is individualized after carefully evaluating the patient's neurological condition, imaging findings, overall health, and risk of future bleeding.

Microsurgical Aneurysm Clipping

Aneurysm Clipping Surgery Bangalore is a well-established microsurgical procedure performed to permanently isolate the aneurysm from the normal circulation.

During surgery:

  • A small opening is made in the skull (craniotomy).
  • The aneurysm is carefully identified using an operating microscope.
  • A specially designed titanium clip is placed across the neck of the aneurysm.
  • Blood flow through the parent artery is preserved while preventing blood from entering the aneurysm.

Microsurgical clipping provides durable, long-term protection against rupture and is particularly effective for many accessible aneurysms.

It is commonly recommended for:

  • Younger patients
  • Broad-neck aneurysms
  • Certain middle cerebral artery aneurysms
  • Complex aneurysms requiring direct visualization

Endovascular Coiling

Endovascular Coiling for Brain Aneurysm Bangalore is a minimally invasive treatment performed through the blood vessels without opening the skull.

During the procedure:

  • A thin catheter is inserted through an artery, usually in the groin or wrist.
  • Under continuous imaging guidance, the catheter is advanced to the brain aneurysm.
  • Soft platinum coils are placed inside the aneurysm.
  • These coils promote clot formation within the aneurysm, preventing blood from entering it.

Potential benefits include:

  • No large surgical incision
  • Shorter hospital stay
  • Faster recovery
  • Less postoperative discomfort
  • Effective treatment for many appropriately selected aneurysms

The choice between clipping and coiling depends on the aneurysm's anatomy and individual patient factors.

Clipping vs Endovascular Coiling

Both procedures are highly effective, but each has specific advantages depending on the aneurysm.

01

Microsurgical Clipping

  • Directly seals the aneurysm
  • Excellent long-term durability
  • Suitable for many broad-neck aneurysms
  • Often preferred for younger patients
02

Endovascular Coiling

  • Minimally invasive
  • No craniotomy required
  • Faster recovery
  • Particularly suitable for selected aneurysms in deeper or surgically challenging locations

Treatment for Ruptured Brain Aneurysm

Ruptured Brain Aneurysm Treatment Bangalore focuses on:

What is an Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM)?

An Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM) is an abnormal connection between arteries and veins in the brain. Unlike normal blood vessels, AVMs bypass the tiny capillaries that regulate blood flow, creating high-pressure blood vessels that are more likely to bleed.

Although AVMs and aneurysms are different conditions, both involve abnormalities of the brain's blood vessels and require specialized neurovascular evaluation.

Patients searching for AVM Treatment in Bangalore should undergo comprehensive imaging to determine the size, location, and bleeding risk of the malformation.

Symptoms of Brain AVM

Some AVMs produce no symptoms until they bleed.

Possible symptoms include:

  • Persistent headaches
  • Seizures
  • Weakness
  • Vision problems
  • Difficulty speaking
  • Numbness
  • Brain haemorrhage
  • Stroke-like symptoms

Diagnosis of AVM

Diagnosis may involve:

  • MRI Brain
  • CT Brain
  • CT Angiography
  • MR Angiography
  • Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA)

These investigations help evaluate the AVM's size, location, blood supply, and drainage pattern.

Available treatments include:

Every treatment plan is individualized to minimize the risk of bleeding while preserving normal brain function.

What is a brain aneurysm?

A brain aneurysm is a weakened area in the wall of a brain artery that bulges outward like a balloon. If it ruptures, it can cause bleeding around the brain and requires immediate emergency treatment.

What causes a brain aneurysm?

Brain aneurysms may develop due to a combination of factors, including high blood pressure, smoking, ageing, family history, connective tissue disorders, and certain congenital blood vessel abnormalities.

What are the symptoms of a ruptured brain aneurysm?

A ruptured aneurysm typically causes a sudden, severe headache often described as the "worst headache of life." Other symptoms include vomiting, neck stiffness, loss of consciousness, seizures, blurred vision, weakness, confusion, and difficulty speaking.

Does every brain aneurysm require surgery?

No. Small, stable aneurysms with a low risk of rupture may be monitored with periodic imaging. Treatment is recommended when the aneurysm has a higher likelihood of rupturing or is causing symptoms.

What is aneurysm clipping surgery?

Aneurysm clipping surgery is a microsurgical procedure in which a small titanium clip is placed across the neck of the aneurysm to permanently prevent blood from entering it while preserving normal blood flow through the artery.

What is endovascular coiling?

Endovascular coiling is a minimally invasive procedure in which platinum coils are inserted into the aneurysm through a catheter placed inside a blood vessel. The coils block blood flow into the aneurysm and reduce the risk of rupture.

Which treatment is better: clipping or coiling?

Neither treatment is universally better. The choice depends on the aneurysm's size, location, shape, rupture status, patient age, and overall health. Your neurosurgeon will recommend the most appropriate option after detailed evaluation.

What is an AVM?

An Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM) is an abnormal connection between arteries and veins in the brain. Unlike an aneurysm, an AVM is a network of abnormal blood vessels that can increase the risk of bleeding or seizures if left untreated.

How long does recovery take after brain aneurysm treatment?

Recovery varies depending on whether the aneurysm was ruptured and the treatment performed. Many patients treated for unruptured aneurysms resume routine activities within a few weeks, while recovery after a ruptured aneurysm may take several months and may involve rehabilitation.

Can a treated brain aneurysm come back?

Most clipped aneurysms are permanently treated. Patients who undergo endovascular coiling may require periodic follow-up imaging because a small number of aneurysms can reopen over time.

When should I seek a second opinion?

A second opinion is recommended if you have been diagnosed with a brain aneurysm, advised to undergo surgery, or would like to understand all available treatment options before making a decision.
