Overview

What is Spine Fusion?

Spinal fusion joins two or more vertebrae so they heal into a single solid bone. It stops painful movement at an unstable segment and protects the nerves.

When it's needed

Signs you may need this treatment.

Having one of these does not always mean surgery. A careful assessment decides the right next step.

01

Spondylolisthesis with nerve compression

02

Instability after fractures, infection or tumours

03

Severe degenerative disc disease

04

Spinal deformity

The procedure

How Dr Sachin approaches it.

Every step is planned around your scans, your symptoms and your goals.

STEP 01

Dynamic X-rays, MRI and CT planning

STEP 02

Decompression of the nerves

STEP 03

Screws and a cage placed, often minimally invasively

STEP 04

Bone graft to fuse the segment over months

Recovery

Back to your life.

Walking starts the next day. Bone fusion matures over 3–6 months, with activity increased step by step.

Walking early

You will be helped to sit up and walk as soon as it is safe, usually within a day.

Wound care and review

Simple dressing care at home, with a follow-up visit to check healing and progress.

Know the warning signs

Call straight away for fever, new weakness, a severe headache, or fluid leaking from the wound.

FAQs

Common questions.

Every case is different. Your own questions are answered at your consultation.

Is spine fusion safe?

Modern neurosurgery uses navigation, magnification and nerve monitoring to keep operations as safe as possible. Dr Sachin explains your personal risks and benefits in detail before any decision is made.

How long does recovery take?

Walking starts the next day. Bone fusion matures over 3–6 months, with activity increased step by step.

Will I definitely need surgery?

Not always. Many conditions improve with medicines, physiotherapy or careful monitoring. Surgery is advised only when it clearly offers you a better outcome.

Can I share my MRI before visiting?

Yes. Send your reports and scan images on WhatsApp and you will be guided on whether an in-person consultation is needed.
